The beautiful game
in full colour.

Mexico hosted the 1970 World Cup, the first widely broadcast in colour. Brazil won all six matches and claimed its third title, led by Pelé and an attacking cast that included Jairzinho, Gérson, Tostão and Rivellino.

Heat and altitude tested the players, while colour television carried the Mexican stadiums to new audiences. Brazil's group meeting with England produced Gordon Banks's remarkable save from Pelé's downward header, one of the tournament's most enduring images.

Brazil beat England 1–0 and continued through the knockout rounds. Jairzinho scored in every Brazilian match, a feat unique to this title run. Elsewhere, Gerd Müller led the tournament with ten goals as West Germany eliminated England in the quarter-finals.

Italy then beat West Germany 4–3 after extra time in a semi-final with five goals beyond the regulation ninety minutes. In the Mexico City final, Pelé headed Brazil ahead. Italy equalised, but the Brazilian attack regained control after the break.

Goals from Gérson, Jairzinho and Carlos Alberto made it 4–1. Brazil kept the Jules Rimet Trophy after its third title, and Carlos Alberto's finish to a sweeping team move became a lasting image of the champions.
