The first home crown
sets the tone.

Italy hosted the second World Cup in 1934 and won the first title decided by a straight knockout tournament. Sixteen teams entered the final stage, with no group games or second chances. Defending champion Uruguay did not take part.

The tournament unfolded before large Italian crowds under Benito Mussolini's regime, which used the event for political display. Every match carried immediate elimination, lending even the opening round a different tension from the competition in Uruguay four years earlier.

Egypt became the first African team to play at a World Cup, while Brazil and the United States went out in their opening matches. Italy advanced past the United States and Spain, but needed a replay after its first quarter-final against the Spanish ended level.

The hosts edged Austria 1–0 in the semi-final. Czechoslovakia reached the Rome final after beating Germany 3–1, with Oldřich Nejedlý finishing the tournament as its top scorer. Antonín Puč put the Czechoslovaks ahead late in the title match.

Raimundo Orsi equalised for Italy before Angelo Schiavio scored in extra time. The 2–1 victory made Italy the first European world champion and completed a demanding run of close knockout matches.
