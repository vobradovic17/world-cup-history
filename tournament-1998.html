France hosted the first 32-team World Cup in 1998 and won its first title. Croatia finished third in its debut as an independent nation, while Brazil returned to the final before losing 3–0 to the hosts in Paris.

The expanded field brought new combinations of teams and stories. Croatia's run became a source of national pride, and Michael Owen's solo goal against Argentina offered England a brilliant moment before its shootout exit.

Croatia beat Germany 3–0 in the quarter-finals, with Davor Šuker eventually finishing as the tournament's top scorer on six goals. Brazil advanced through the other side of the draw, and Ronaldo's performances earned him the Golden Ball.

France needed Laurent Blanc's golden goal to beat Paraguay and penalties to get past Italy. Lilian Thuram then scored his only two international goals in the semi-final against Croatia, overturning Šuker's opener and sending the hosts to meet Brazil.

Zinédine Zidane headed in twice in the final before Emmanuel Petit completed a 3–0 victory. France's first world title brought a national celebration, with Zidane's goals the clearest images of its triumph.
