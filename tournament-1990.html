A narrow
victory.

Italy hosted the 1990 World Cup, which West Germany won by beating Argentina in a rematch of the previous final. Cameroon supplied the breakthrough story, reaching the quarter-finals, while Italy's Salvatore Schillaci became an unexpected scoring star.

Italian stadiums provided an impressive setting, though many matches were tight and low scoring. Roger Milla's corner-flag celebrations gave Cameroon's run a joyful character that stood out against the caution of several knockout contests.

Cameroon stunned defending champion Argentina in the opener and later eliminated Colombia before England stopped it in a dramatic quarter-final. Schillaci's goals carried the hosts through the draw; he ultimately scored six and received the Golden Ball.

Argentina beat Yugoslavia and then Italy on penalties to reach the final. West Germany also needed a shootout against England in its semi-final. The Rome decider remained tense, and Argentina finished with nine players after two dismissals.

Andreas Brehme converted a late penalty for a 1–0 West German win and a third world title. Cameroon's breakthrough and the narrow final left sharply different memories of the same tournament.
