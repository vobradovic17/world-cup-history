Mexico hosted the 1986 World Cup after Colombia withdrew. Argentina won its second title under Diego Maradona's leadership, while England's Gary Lineker finished as the tournament's leading scorer with six goals.

Huge crowds, especially at the Azteca, gave the competition noise and colour. The altitude and midday heat shaped the experience for players, while a series of dramatic knockout games kept attention on more than the eventual champions.

Denmark's exciting group-stage run ended in a 5–1 defeat by Spain. France beat Brazil on penalties in a tense quarter-final, and Lineker's goals carried England into a meeting with Argentina. Maradona had already established himself as the centre of his side's play.

Against England, Maradona scored first with a handball missed by the officials, then after a breathtaking run from his own half. He scored both goals against Belgium in the semi-final to take Argentina into the final against West Germany.

The Germans recovered from 2–0 down, but Maradona released Jorge Burruchaga for the winning goal in a 3–2 final. Argentina claimed its second world title, and Maradona's two contrasting goals against England became the edition's lasting images.
