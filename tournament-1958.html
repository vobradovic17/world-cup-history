A star
arrives.

Sweden hosted the 1958 World Cup, where Brazil won its first title and 17-year-old Pelé emerged on the global stage. France's Just Fontaine set a record with thirteen goals, while the home side made its own memorable run to the final.

Swedish support grew as the hosts advanced, culminating in a semi-final victory over West Germany. Brazil offered a different attraction: Garrincha's dribbling, Didi's passing and Vavá's finishing surrounded the teenage Pelé with an array of attacking options.

Fontaine scored throughout France's campaign, including four goals in the third-place match. Pelé began making his mark in the knockout rounds, scoring the winner against Wales and then a hat-trick against France in the semi-final.

Sweden took an early lead in the Stockholm final, but Vavá struck twice to turn the game around. Pelé added two goals of his own, one after a deft flick over a defender and a volley, as Brazil pulled clear of the hosts.

Brazil won 5–2 for its first world title. The final crowned a fluent attacking team and began Pelé's extraordinary World Cup story.
