The match
that echoed.

Brazil hosted the World Cup's return in 1950 after a twelve-year interruption caused by the Second World War. The title was decided by a final round-robin group, and Uruguay emerged as champion after beating Brazil in the last match at the Maracanã.

Huge crowds filled Brazil's grounds, especially the newly built Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro. The home team's prolific attack raised expectations with every round. By the last day, the stadium had become the setting for what many supporters expected to be a coronation.

The United States delivered an early shock by beating England 1–0. Brazil later overwhelmed Sweden 7–1 and Spain 6–1 in the final group, while Ademir de Menezes finished as the tournament's leading scorer. Uruguay had to battle through its own group matches to stay in contention.

Brazil needed only a draw in the final game and went ahead through Friaça. Uruguay, which had to win, equalised through Juan Alberto Schiaffino. With little time remaining, Alcides Ghiggia scored again and silenced the vast home crowd.

Uruguay's 2–1 victory brought a second world title. The reversal, remembered as the Maracanazo, remains one of the competition's most powerful examples of how quickly a seemingly certain outcome can change.
