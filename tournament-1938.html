A title defended
away from home.

France hosted the 1938 World Cup, another straight knockout edition. Italy arrived as defending champion and became the first team to retain the trophy, beating Hungary in the Paris final. Brazil's adventurous attack supplied several of the tournament's most dramatic moments.

The French grounds saw open, high-scoring football. Brazil and Poland produced an extraordinary 6–5 match in Strasbourg, with the lead repeatedly under threat. The contest captured the unpredictable mood of an edition in which several teams scored freely.

Brazil's Leônidas led the scorers with seven goals as his team progressed through the draw. Italy took a steadier route, eliminating host France in the quarter-finals and relying on the finishing of Silvio Piola and Gino Colaussi.

Italy then beat Brazil 2–1 in the semi-final. Hungary reached the final from the other side of the bracket and replied quickly after Italy opened the scoring, but Colaussi and Piola each struck twice to keep the champions in command.

Italy won 4–2 and became the first side to win consecutive World Cups. War prevented another tournament until 1950, so the Paris final also closed the competition's first era.
