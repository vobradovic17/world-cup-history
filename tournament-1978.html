A nation
finds its voice.

Argentina hosted and won the 1978 World Cup, claiming its first title against the Netherlands. Mario Kempes became the tournament's leading scorer, while the event unfolded under the country's military dictatorship.

Blue-and-white paper filled Argentine stadiums and home crowds followed the team closely. The military government also sought to use the competition for its own image, a political setting that remains part of the edition's history even in accounts of the celebrations.

Argentina advanced from the first group stage alongside Italy. Kempes did not score in that opening phase, but began finding the net in the second. Elsewhere, Scotland's Archie Gemmill scored a memorable solo goal in a 3–2 win over the Netherlands, though his side still went out.

Kempes scored twice as Argentina beat Poland, then twice more in the 6–0 win over Peru that sent the hosts to the final. The Netherlands reached a second consecutive title match and drew level in Buenos Aires through Dick Nanninga after Kempes had opened the scoring.

Rob Rensenbrink hit the post late in normal time, but Kempes struck again in extra time before Daniel Bertoni sealed a 3–1 win. Argentina celebrated its first world title in an atmosphere inseparable from the country's political crisis.
