Brazil hosted the 2014 World Cup, but Germany won the title after delivering the hosts a devastating semi-final defeat. Argentina reached the final, while Colombia and Costa Rica produced memorable runs through the knockout stage.

Colourful home crowds gave the tournament energy, even as Brazil's expectations grew heavier. Colombia's James Rodríguez drew attention with his goals and celebrations, and Costa Rica's progress challenged pre-tournament assumptions about the field.

Costa Rica won a group containing Uruguay, Italy and England and went on to the quarter-finals. Rodríguez scored six times, including a spectacular turning volley against Uruguay. Brazil advanced too, but Neymar's injury in the quarter-final left the hosts without their leading attacker.

Germany then beat Brazil 7–1 in a semi-final that stunned the country; Miroslav Klose became the World Cup's all-time leading scorer during the match. Argentina beat the Netherlands on penalties to set up a final between the South American side and Germany.

In Rio de Janeiro, Mario Götze controlled André Schürrle's cross and scored in extra time for a 1–0 German win. Germany became the first European side to win a World Cup in the Americas, although the 7–1 semi-final remained the edition's most startling result.
