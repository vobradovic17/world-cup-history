Russia hosted the 2018 World Cup, the first to use video assistant refereeing. France won its second title, beating Croatia in the final, while defending champion Germany exited in the group stage.

Packed Russian stadiums witnessed repeated swings in momentum. Belgium's comeback against Japan and England's renewed run to the semi-finals kept the knockout rounds lively, alongside the long series of close matches survived by Croatia.

Germany's early elimination opened the field. France beat Argentina 4–3 in the round of 16, with teenage Kylian Mbappé scoring twice. Belgium recovered from 2–0 down to beat Japan 3–2, then eliminated Brazil in the quarter-finals.

Croatia advanced past Denmark and Russia on penalties, then beat England in extra time to reach its first final. Luka Modrić directed the midfield throughout that demanding run, while France overcame Belgium in the other semi-final.

An own goal, an Antoine Griezmann penalty and strikes from Paul Pogba and Mbappé gave France a 4–2 win in Moscow. Its second title crowned a new generation, and Modrić's Croatia left with a historic runner-up finish.
