Football comes
home.

England hosted the 1966 World Cup and won its first title, defeating West Germany after extra time at Wembley. Sixteen teams competed, with Portugal and North Korea among the sides that gave the edition some of its most surprising results.

The tournament began amid an unlikely off-field drama: a dog named Pickles found the stolen Jules Rimet Trophy before a ball was kicked. As England advanced, Wembley became the centre of home expectation, and the packed final gave the hosts their largest stage.

North Korea eliminated Italy in the group stage and then raced into a 3–0 quarter-final lead against Portugal. Eusébio scored four times as Portugal recovered to win 5–3. His nine goals made him the tournament's leading scorer.

England reached the semi-final with a strong defence, then beat Portugal 2–1 through two Bobby Charlton goals. West Germany awaited at Wembley. Geoff Hurst and Martin Peters scored for England, but Wolfgang Weber's late equaliser made it 2–2 and forced extra time.

Hurst's next shot hit the bar and bounced near the line; the referee awarded the disputed goal. Hurst completed his hat-trick as England won 4–2. The decision and the home side's first world title remain inseparable from the story of the final.
