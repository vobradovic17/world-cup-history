A new century
starts here.

The 2030 World Cup is planned as a centenary edition, one hundred years after the first tournament in Uruguay. Morocco, Portugal and Spain are the principal hosts, with one celebratory match each in Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay.

The opening plan gives supporters in South America a direct connection to 1930. Montevideo, where every match of the first World Cup was played, will host a centenary celebration, before the event moves to stadiums across North Africa and Europe.

Three centenary matches are scheduled for Uruguay, Argentina and Paraguay. Uruguay's game will return the competition to Montevideo, while Argentina's role recalls its appearance in the first final and Paraguay's reflects its place in South American football history.

After those centenary games, play is due to continue in Morocco, Portugal and Spain. FIFA plans extra travel and rest time for teams involved in the South American matches, allowing them to cross the Atlantic before their next fixtures.

No matches have been played and no champion can be named. The tournament's defining results will emerge in 2030; for now, the six-country plan offers a way to mark a century of World Cup football.
