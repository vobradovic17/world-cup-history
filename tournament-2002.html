The fifth star
under new lights.

South Korea and Japan co-hosted the 2002 World Cup, the first staged in Asia. Brazil won all seven matches to claim a fifth title, while Senegal and South Korea delivered two of the competition's most surprising runs.

Red-clad crowds followed South Korea from city to city, giving the co-hosts' campaign a striking visual presence. New stadiums and unfamiliar settings broadened the tournament's reach, and Senegal's debut added another source of excitement after the opening match.

Senegal stunned defending champion France 1–0 and went on to the quarter-finals. South Korea eliminated Italy with a golden goal, then beat Spain on penalties to reach the semi-finals, where Germany finally stopped its run.

Brazil's attack combined Ronaldo's finishing with Rivaldo's movement and Ronaldinho's creativity. Ronaldo scored in the semi-final against Türkiye to set up a final with Germany, whose goalkeeper Oliver Kahn had been central to its own progress.

Ronaldo struck twice in Yokohama for a 2–0 win and finished with eight goals. Brazil took a record fifth crown, and his return to the final four years after the troubled 1998 decider supplied a fitting close.
