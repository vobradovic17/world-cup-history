A new champion
for a new continent.

South Africa hosted the first World Cup on African soil in 2010. Spain recovered from an opening defeat to win its first title, beating the Netherlands after extra time. Ghana's near miss in the quarter-finals supplied another defining story.

Vuvuzelas sounded through packed stadiums and made the tournament instantly recognisable. South African crowds welcomed teams from around the world, while Ghana's progress carried special hopes for an African semi-finalist.

Germany scored four against both England and Argentina, and Ghana reached the last eight against Uruguay. Luis Suárez handled a goal-bound shot near the end of extra time; Asamoah Gyan missed the resulting penalty and Uruguay won the shootout.

Spain steadied itself after losing to Switzerland, then beat Portugal, Paraguay and Germany by 1–0 scores. The Netherlands arrived at the Johannesburg final unbeaten and nearly went ahead when Arjen Robben broke through, but Iker Casillas saved.

Andrés Iniesta scored in the 116th minute for a 1–0 victory. Spain's patient run ended with its first world title and provided a dramatic close to the tournament's African debut.
