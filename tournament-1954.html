A comeback
for the ages.

Switzerland hosted the 1954 World Cup, a high-scoring edition that ended with West Germany's first title. Hungary entered the final unbeaten for years and had already beaten the Germans 8–3 in the group stage, making the result in Bern an immense upset.

Goals came in extraordinary numbers across the Swiss grounds. Austria's 7–5 quarter-final win over the hosts remains the highest-scoring match in World Cup history. Even strong leads could feel fragile, creating a restless atmosphere around the tournament's attacking sides.

Hungary began by scoring nine against South Korea and eight against West Germany. Sándor Kocsis finished with eleven goals, while Ferenc Puskás and his teammates survived demanding 4–2 knockout wins over Brazil and Uruguay to reach the final.

West Germany recovered from its heavy group defeat to make the same match. In rain-soaked Bern, Hungary went 2–0 ahead inside eight minutes. Max Morlock and Helmut Rahn quickly drew the Germans level before Rahn struck again late in the second half.

The 3–2 comeback became the Miracle of Bern. West Germany lifted its first World Cup, while Hungary's only defeat in the competition came in the match that decided the title.
