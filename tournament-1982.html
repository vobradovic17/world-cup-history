Three goals
and a third star.

Spain hosted the first 24-team World Cup in 1982. Italy recovered from three opening draws to win a third title, with Paolo Rossi's late burst of goals transforming its campaign. The expanded format also brought memorable upsets and a dispute over the final first-round group games.

The Spanish edition swung between attacking spectacle and frustration. Hungary beat El Salvador 10–1, while Algeria defeated West Germany and Chile yet was eliminated after West Germany's narrow win over Austria. The controversy led to simultaneous final group kickoffs in later editions.

Italy advanced despite its three draws and found form in the second group phase. Rossi scored a hat-trick in a 3–2 win over a Brazil side led by Sócrates, Zico and Falcão, abruptly ending one of the tournament's most admired attacking runs.

Rossi then scored twice against Poland in the semi-final. West Germany survived a 3–3 semi-final against France on penalties, setting up the Madrid final. Rossi arrived there with five goals from Italy's previous two matches and the scoring lead within reach.

Rossi broke the deadlock, and Marco Tardelli and Alessandro Altobelli extended Italy's lead before West Germany replied. The 3–1 victory gave Italy its third title; Rossi finished with six goals and Tardelli's exuberant celebration became a defining image.
