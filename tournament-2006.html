A final
of nerve.

Germany hosted the 2006 World Cup, which Italy won for the fourth time. France reached the final through a strong knockout run led by Zinédine Zidane, while the hosts' own progress helped sustain a lively public festival.

Supporters filled fan zones and city squares across Germany. The host team's matches kept those public viewing areas busy into July, and the welcoming mood extended beyond the stadiums even as the competition tightened in its later rounds.

Miroslav Klose scored five times for Germany to lead the tournament. France came through Spain, Brazil and Portugal, with Zidane directing its play. Italy advanced with a defence that conceded only twice in seven matches.

Italy's semi-final against Germany remained scoreless until two late extra-time goals settled it. In the Berlin final, Zidane's chipped penalty put France ahead, but Marco Materazzi equalised. Deep in extra time Zidane was sent off for headbutting Materazzi.

Italy won the shootout 5–3 and claimed its fourth world title. The victory completed a disciplined team campaign, while Zidane's dismissal gave his last professional match a shocking final scene.
