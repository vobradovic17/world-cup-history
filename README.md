# World Cup History Repo

This is a companion repo for the [worldcuphistory.com](https://worldcuphistory.com/) website.

## Player record profiles

`data/world-cup-players-highlighted.json` retains all 118 player and coach profiles. The visible record sections use these profiles by name; eight profiles remain in the data file even though no current record row references them. Every entry uses the same `name`, `latest_highlight_year`, `awards`, `final_goals`, and `profile` fields. Run `python3 build_players.py` after editing this file.

The build creates `players.html` and one `player-*.html` page per profile. The record panels on `players.html` link to those pages. Each profile page shows the full biography, portrait credit, World Cup honours, and match archive. Player campaigns use the `matches` arrays below; winning coaches also have title-run match sections built from the tournament match files. The shared profile hero image is an AI-generated illustrative football scene at `assets/player-page-hero.webp`.

Every profile also has `world_cups_played`, `matches_played`, `matches`, and `goals_scored`. Run `python3 update_player_stats.py` to recalculate these from the `world-cup-YYYY-matches.json` files. Each `matches` entry records the year, date, stage, teams, home/away score, whether the player started, and the complete `lineup`, `substitutions`, and `scorers` for both teams; substitute appearances also record the minute they entered. The updater verifies that the number of entries equals `matches_played`. A World Cup counts when the person played in at least one match; match totals include starts and substitute appearances; goal totals exclude own goals and shootouts. These are playing statistics, so coaches with no World Cup match as a player have zeroes and empty `matches` arrays.

`best_placement` uses the same `{ "placement", "years" }` format as the team directory, restricted to tournaments in which the person played. Coach-only profiles have `null` because this field describes playing results.

Goalkeeper profiles also have `clean_sheets`, counting matches they played in which their team conceded no goals. The updater identifies goalkeepers from the first lineup position and follows goalkeeper substitutions.

Player and coach portraits are indexed in `assets/player-portraits/sources.json`; creator and license details are stored in `assets/player-portraits/credits.json` and listed in the page's Photo credits section. The local portraits are WebP conversions of the linked source files; some are cropped to keep faces visible.

The record profiles were checked against the tournament and award files in `data/` and FIFA's [World Cup champions history](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/world-cup-champions-1930-1978-uruguay-italy-germany-brazil-england-argentina), [all-time scoring list](https://www.fifa.com/en/id/mens/worldcup/articles/fifa-world-cup-all-time-leading-scorers), [appearance records](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/players-most-appearances-games-matches-history), [Golden Glove winners](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/canadamexicousa2026/articles/unai-simon-adidas-golden-glove), and [2026 award winners](https://www.fifa.com/mens/articles/award-winners). Young-player entries before 2006 are retrospective selections, as recorded in the local award data.

`awards` mirrors the local tournament top scorer, best player, best young player, and best goalkeeper lists for every profile. Retrospective and Golden Ball selections retain their source tags. Winning captains and coaches have their own record sections and are not counted as individual awards. `final_goals` records goals by [Zagallo in 1958](https://www.fifa.com/pt/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/brasil-suecia-final-copa-1958), [Carlos Alberto in 1970](https://www.fifa.com/es/tournaments/mens/worldcup/canadamexicousa2026/articles/carlos-alberto-brasil-italia-1970-copa-del-mundo), and [Ivan Perišić in 2018](https://inside.fifa.com/fr/tournaments/mens/worldcup/2018russia/news/les-bleus-decrochent-leur-deuxieme-etoile). Empty arrays mean that no achievement or final goal of these types is recorded for that person.

## Tournament pages

Each edition has a standalone `tournament-YYYY.html` page. Match results, dates, and scores come from its file in `data/`. `data/tournament-editorial.json` holds the short editorial summaries and page facts; `data/tournament-page-stories.json` holds the five-paragraph narratives shown only on the tournament pages. The landing-page summaries remain in `script.js`. Run `python3 build_tournaments.py` after changing page data or stories to rebuild all pages. The 2030 file records an upcoming edition with no results.

The longer narratives were checked against [FIFA's history of champions from 1930 to 1978](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/world-cup-champions-1930-1978-uruguay-italy-germany-brazil-england-argentina), [the history from 1982 to 2026](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/world-cup-champions-1982-2026-italy-argentina-germany-brazil-france-spain), [the 2026 final report](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/canadamexicousa2026/articles/spain-argentina-final-report-highlights), and [FIFA's 2030 hosting plan](https://inside.fifa.com/es/tournament-organisation/world-cup-2030). The match archive in this repository supports the scores and dates mentioned in them.

Each entry in `data/tournament-editorial.json` also records `topScorer` (goals and every player tied for the lead), `bestPlayer` (Golden Ball), `bestYoungPlayer`, and `bestGoalkeeper` (Yashin Award or Golden Glove). Young-player selections for 1958–2002 were made retrospectively and carry `selection: "retrospective"`; recipients from 2006 onward carry `selection: "official"`. `null` means the award was not presented for that edition, or that the 2030 tournament has not been played. Sources: FIFA's [top-scorer history](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/canadamexicousa2026/articles/golden-boot-top-goalscorer), [Golden Ball winners](https://www.fifa.com/en/articles/rodri-earns-adidas-golden-ball), [complete Young Player history](https://www.fifa.com/es/articles/pau-cubarsi-premio-fifa-jugador-joven), and [Golden Glove winners](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/canadamexicousa2026/articles/unai-simon-adidas-golden-glove).

Stadium details for 2018 and 2022 were matched to the [2018](https://fixturedownload.com/results/fifa-world-cup-2018) and [2022](https://fixturedownload.com/results/fifa-world-cup-2022) fixture lists. The 2026 host-stadium names follow [FIFA's venue list](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/canadamexicousa2026/articles/fifa-world-cup-2026-hosts-cities-dates-usa-mexico-canada).

Every played match has `lineup.home` and `lineup.away` arrays of eleven starters, a `substitutions` array, and a `scorers` array. Substitutions record `team`, `player_on`, `player_off`, and `minute`; scorer records use `team`, `player`, and `minute`, with `penalty` or `own_goal` when applicable. Minutes are strings so added time can be represented as `90+3`. The 1930–1966 tournaments have no in-match substitutions. The 2030 file has no matches yet. Lineups, substitutions, and scorers through 2022 were checked against the public-domain [openfootball World Cup archive](https://github.com/openfootball/worldcup.json); each tournament JSON lists its source file. One incomplete source lineup in the 1954 Turkey–South Korea match uses the existing local eleven instead.

The tournament hero images in `assets/tournament-YYYY-hero.webp` are AI-generated illustrative reconstructions of specific editions. They are labelled as such on the pages and should not be presented as archival photographs.
